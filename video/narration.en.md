One guest changes their plans. For a small hotel, that can change the room, the cleaning schedule, the linen, breakfast, and the team's day. StayWeave connects those decisions in one place.

Here, I enter two new requests. One guest requires a sea view. The other prefers one, but is open to alternatives. These words go to an actual NVIDIA Nemotron model through Nebius Token Factory.

The model separates required conditions from preferences. We show the model used, the measured response time, and estimated inference cost. These are measurements from the call, not an animation timer.

Then our coordination engine checks the requests together. It considers eligible rooms and operating resources, while protecting existing reservations. The owner can see which room is proposed, why it fits, and what would change.

A proposal is not a booking. The customer chooses the current terms first. Only then do the reservation and connected operating plans update. The dashboard shows the actual difference. A ready room does not create unnecessary cleaning work.

If a guest declines, StayWeave rechecks the alternatives. It does not overwrite another guest's confirmed booking. Separate local scenarios demonstrate cancellations and resource shortages, with decisions outside the owner's policy sent for review.

The division of work is deliberate. AI interprets intent. Code validates availability and resources. Customers consent. The system records the resulting changes.

The channel-labelled inbox brings the work into one operating view. Today, external booking channels are simulated. Authorized platform integrations are the next step, alongside pilots measuring owner interventions and verified delivery.

StayWeave turns guest intent into a coordinated hotel plan, so owners can focus on the exceptions that need their judgment.
